Best AI Podcast Editing Tools (2026)

30 September 2026

Best AI podcast editing tools have become the backbone of modern audio production, letting solo creators and small teams cut hours of manual work down to minutes. In this roundup, we look at the strongest options in 2026, what each does well, and where they fall short — so you can pick based on your workflow, not marketing hype.

Why use AI for podcast editing?

Traditional podcast editing means scrubbing through waveforms, removing filler words by hand, leveling audio clip by clip, and cleaning up noise. AI tools now automate most of that: they transcribe your raw audio, detect and delete “ums” and long pauses, remove background noise and room echo, and even generate show notes and social clips from the same session.

The catch: no tool is fully hands-off. Every option below still benefits from a human pass, especially for narrative or interview-heavy shows where automated cuts can flatten pacing or remove intentional pauses.

1. Descript

Descript remains the most complete AI podcast editing software in 2026. Its core idea is simple but powerful: your audio is transcribed, and you edit the transcript like a document. Delete a sentence, and the corresponding audio goes with it.

Key strengths:

  • Text-based editing — remove filler words (“um,” “uh,” “you know”) across an entire episode in one click.
  • Studio Sound — AI voice enhancement that cleans up cheap mics and noisy rooms remarkably well.
  • Overdub — a voice clone that lets you fix flubbed lines by typing corrected text.
  • Extras — automatic show notes, chapter markers, and clip generation for social media.

Weaknesses: transcription accuracy dips with heavy accents, crosstalk, or jargon, so always proofread before editing. The interface is dense, and some users find the word-based editing awkward for music-heavy or narrative shows.

2. Adobe Podcast (Enhance Speech)

Adobe Podcast isn’t a full editing suite — it’s a free-tier web tool focused on one thing: making bad audio sound good. Upload a file, and its AI removes background noise, room echo, and hum while boosting voice clarity.

Where it shines: recorded-on-a-laptop interviews, remote guests with poor setups, and rescue jobs where re-recording isn’t possible. It won’t fix everything — over-processed audio can sound slightly “distant” — but as a free cleanup pass, it’s hard to beat. Adobe also continues to fold podcast-relevant features into its broader AI toolset, much like the AI video editors we compared in our best AI video editors guide.

3. Riverside

Riverside started as a remote recording platform and has grown into a solid AI-assisted editing workflow. Its big advantage is recording: it captures each participant’s audio and video locally in high quality, so bad Wi-Fi doesn’t ruin your raw files.

On the AI side, Riverside now offers automatic transcription, silence and filler-word removal, text-based editing, and Magic Clips for turning long episodes into short social videos. If your bottleneck is recording quality and editing time, Riverside covers both in one subscription. If you only need editing, Descript is more mature.

4. Podcastle

Podcastle targets beginners and budget-conscious creators. It bundles recording, an AI-powered editor, noise reduction, text-based editing, and even AI-generated voices into a single web app. It also has a free tier that’s genuinely usable for short episodes.

The trade-off is polish and depth: fewer advanced features than Descript, less refined audio processing, and occasional quirks in the editor. But for someone launching a first show — or a hobbyist who doesn’t want to pay pro-tier prices — it’s a sensible starting point. Check current pricing before committing, as tiers change frequently.

5. Auphonic

Auphonic is the veteran of automated audio post-production. It doesn’t edit content — there’s no timeline or transcript editing — but it excels at mastering: automatic loudness normalization to podcast standards, leveling between speakers, noise and hum reduction, and encoding/export presets.

Many creators use Auphonic as the final step regardless of which editor they cut in. If you publish on a schedule and just want consistent, broadcast-compliant loudness without touching a fader, this is the tool. It’s web-based, with a monthly free allowance of processing hours.

Honorable mentions

  • Alitu — a very beginner-friendly “podcast maker” with automated cleanup and one-click publishing; less control, less fuss.
  • Swell AI / similar repurposing tools — not editors, but useful for auto-generating show notes, titles, and transcripts from your finished audio. For meeting-style transcription needs, see our Otter.ai review.
  • Hindenburg — not AI-first, but its auto-leveling and spoken-word focus make it a favorite among journalists who want minimal manual mixing.

How to choose

Ask yourself three questions:

  1. What’s your biggest time sink? If it’s cutting filler words and restructuring takes, go text-based (Descript or Riverside). If it’s audio quality, prioritize Adobe Podcast or Auphonic. If it’s show notes and promotion, add a repurposing tool.
  2. Do you record remotely? Riverside’s local recording is a genuine differentiator for interview shows.
  3. What’s your budget? Adobe Podcast and Podcastle have usable free tiers; Descript and Riverside are subscription products with pricing that varies by plan — check current pricing before deciding.

A common 2026 stack looks like this: Riverside or your DAW for recording, Descript for editing, Auphonic for final mastering, and a repurposing tool for notes and clips. That’s more subscriptions than some people want, but each covers a gap the others don’t.

Who this is for

  • Solo podcasters and hobbyists: Podcastle or Adobe Podcast — low cost, low complexity.
  • Interview shows with remote guests: Riverside for recording plus editing in one place.
  • Creators who want maximum control and speed: Descript, ideally paired with Auphonic for mastering.
  • Shows with consistent publishing schedules: Auphonic as a set-and-forget mastering layer.

Alternatives worth knowing

  • Traditional DAWs (Reaper, Audacity, Adobe Audition): more control, no AI automation. Reaper is inexpensive and beloved; Audition integrates with Adobe’s ecosystem. If AI features frustrate you, these remain the fallback.
  • AI writing tools for show notes: if your main need is scripts, episode descriptions, and repurposing, our best AI writing tools comparison covers options that pair well with any editor above.
  • Human editors: for narrative or documentary-style podcasts, a skilled human editor still outperforms every tool here. AI gets you 80% there; the last 20% of pacing and emotional beats is still a craft.

Final verdict

For most podcasters in 2026, Descript is the best overall AI podcast editing software — its text-based workflow genuinely changes how fast you can produce an episode. Riverside is the pick if remote recording matters, Podcastle if budget does, and Auphonic deserves a spot in almost everyone’s pipeline for mastering. Whichever you choose, treat AI as an accelerator, not a replacement for a final listen-through — your ears are still the best quality-control tool you have.
