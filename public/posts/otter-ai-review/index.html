Otter.ai Review: Best Meeting Transcription?

30 September 2026

Otter.ai is the default answer to “record and transcribe my meetings automatically.” It joins Zoom, Meet, and Teams calls, produces a searchable transcript, and summarizes action items. The question is not whether it works — it does — but whether you need a paid plan.

What it does well

  • Automated note-taking: the OtterPilot bot joins your calls and produces a transcript without you doing anything.
  • Search and recall: everything is full-text searchable. Finding “what did we agree on pricing three weeks ago” takes seconds.
  • Action-item extraction: the AI summary picks out owners and deadlines with decent accuracy on clearly spoken plans.
  • Collaboration: highlights, comments, and shared channels work well for team meeting hygiene.

Where it falls short

  • Accuracy drops with accents, jargon, and crosstalk. Expect to skim and correct; it is a draft, not a record.
  • Privacy optics: an AI participant inside your call requires telling attendees — configure disclosure carefully.
  • Monthly transcription caps on free and lower tiers fill up fast if you take many calls.
  • Editing in the web app is clunkier than a purpose-built editor for long-form cleanup.

Pricing

Free tier gives a monthly transcription allotment with the bot restricted; paid tiers raise limits and unlock advanced features. Check Otter’s current pricing page — plans change a couple of times per year. [#AFF-LINK-otter]

Who this is for

  • Buy it: you attend 5+ meetings/week and need searchable notes and follow-ups.
  • Skip it: your meetings are mostly one-on-ones you summarize yourself, or your employer prohibits external meeting bots.

Alternatives

  • Fireflies — similar scope, stronger search, different caps.
  • tl;dv — better highlight clips for async teams.
  • Whisper-based local tools — free and private, but you self-host and lose the meeting-bot convenience.

Verdict

For meeting-heavy roles Otter is the lowest-friction transcription choice, and the free tier is a genuinely useful trial. [#AFF-LINK-otter]
